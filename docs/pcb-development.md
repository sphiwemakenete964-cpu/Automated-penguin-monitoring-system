\# PCB Development Process



\## 1. Introduction



The PCB development formed part of my contribution to the Automated Penguin Monitoring System (APMS).



The circuit schematic was supplied by the supervisor/project team. My responsibility was to implement the existing circuit as a PCB layout without changing the supplied circuit design.



\## 2. Development Environment



The PCB layout was developed using:



\- KiCad 10



\## 3. PCB Development Activities



\### Component Footprint Assignment



Appropriate PCB footprints were assigned to the components in the supplied circuit.



\### Component Placement



Components were arranged on the PCB while considering board space, connectivity and physical layout requirements.



\### Track Routing



Tracks were routed to establish the required electrical connections between components.



\### Copper Zones



Copper zones and pours were used as part of the PCB layout.



\### Mechanical Considerations



Board dimensions and mounting-hole placement were considered during the layout process.



\### Design Rule Checks



PCB design-rule issues, including clearance violations and zone connectivity problems, were investigated during development.



\## 4. Scope of My Contribution



My work focused on PCB implementation and layout.



The original circuit schematic and its electrical connections were kept unchanged, as instructed by the supervisor/project team.





\---



\## 5. Engineering Challenges



During PCB development, several technical issues required investigation.



\### Clearance Violations



PCB clearance constraints had to be considered to prevent tracks and

copper features from being placed too close to one another.



\### Copper Zone Connectivity



Copper zones were configured to provide the required connections.

Issues involving zone connectivity and thermal relief connections

required further investigation.



\### Annular Ring Width



Board design constraints, including annular ring width, were

considered when checking the suitability of PCB footprints.



\### PCB Design Rule Checking



KiCad's Design Rules Checker (DRC) was used to identify potential

layout issues and guide further investigation.



\## 6. Technical Learning Outcomes



This development process provided practical experience in:



\- PCB layout and component placement.

\- Electrical connectivity and copper zone configuration.

\- PCB manufacturing constraints.

\- Design-rule checking and troubleshooting.

\- Translating an existing circuit into a physical PCB layout.



\## PCB Layout Workflow



The PCB development process involved translating the supplied circuit schematic into a physical PCB layout using KiCad 10.



The workflow included:



1\. \*\*Footprint assignment:\*\* Selecting suitable PCB footprints for the components specified in the schematic.

2\. \*\*Component placement:\*\* Arranging components while considering board dimensions, electrical connections, accessibility and practical layout constraints.

3\. \*\*Board routing:\*\* Connecting component pads according to the schematic-defined electrical nets.

4\. \*\*Copper zones:\*\* Using copper zones where appropriate to provide common electrical connections and improve routing efficiency.

5\. \*\*Design rule checking:\*\* Reviewing PCB design-rule-check (DRC) results to identify issues such as clearance violations and incomplete zone connections.

6\. \*\*Layout refinement:\*\* Adjusting component positions, track routing and clearances to improve the overall PCB layout.



This process provided practical experience in PCB implementation, electrical connectivity, component footprint selection and the importance of design-rule verification.
